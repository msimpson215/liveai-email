PHILANTHROPIC CONCEPT

Ronald McDonald Kitchen

Two jobs at once: help hospitalized children actually eat — and generate serious revenue for children’s hospitals and Ronald McDonald House Charities across the United States. Concept estimates in the tens of millions — on the order of $13 million to $34 million raised for hospitals, with a full national September campaign modeled much higher.

WHERE IT CAME FROM

Born at St. Louis Children’s Hospital.

During his daughter’s treatment, Martin saw that cafeteria food could be excellent — but kids still lit up for something familiar, like a Happy Meal. For months he brought a custom wagon with a cooler and warmers to oncology every Saturday so each child got exactly what they ordered. The goal was simple: make sure the children ate, and felt remembered.

Ronald McDonald Kitchen turns that personal tradition into a scalable program: comfort for kids, and real revenue for the hospitals and houses that care for them.

THE DOUBLE PURPOSE

Feed the kids. Fund the hospitals.

Keep children eating

Familiar meals — Happy Meals, cones, nuggets — delivered through hospital kitchen / kiosk access so kids in treatment get comfort food they will actually eat.

Raise money for children’s hospitals

Everyday McDonald’s customers donate a meal or cone at the register. When donations outpace meals given out, excess funds go to children’s hospitals and Ronald McDonald House — a national fundraising engine estimated around $13 million to $34 million for hospitals, with higher upside at full scale.

Self-sustaining model

Franchises keep a small operational incentive per donation. The public gets an easy way to give. Hospitals and RMH receive ongoing support. The children get the meal.

HOW IT WORKS

Donate at McDonald’s. Redeem in the hospital.

1

Customer donates

At checkout or the app: “Would you like to donate a Happy Meal or cone to a child at Children’s Hospital today?” Donor gets a sticker: “I Made a Child Smile Today.”

2

Donation is tracked

Donations are stored digitally through a clearinghouse. Franchises retain a small per-item amount; the rest supports meals and hospital / RMH programs.

3

Child redeems in hospital

Social workers issue meal cards. Kids redeem at a Ronald McDonald Kitchen kiosk inside the hospital cafeteria — free meals for cardholders; anyone can also buy there.

FUNDRAISING SCALE

A huge revenue generator for children’s hospitals.

The point is keeping kids eating — and turning everyday McDonald’s traffic into surplus capital for pediatric hospitals and Ronald McDonald House Charities when donations exceed meals redeemed.

Hospital fundraising estimates

Martin’s working estimates for what the model can raise for children’s hospitals nationwide sit in the $13 million to $34 million range — serious hospital revenue, not a one-off charity ask. Excess Happy Meal and cone donations (after kids are fed and kiosk costs) go to the hospital and Ronald McDonald House.

St. Louis pilot (September)

Mini business plan: ~50 metro McDonald’s, Childhood Cancer Awareness Month. Projected about $528,000 in one September for local children’s hospitals (Children’s Hospital / Cardinal Glennon) and related programs.

Full national September math

Same participation assumptions across roughly 13,000 U.S. McDonald’s locations project about $137 million in a single September. That is the upside case; the $13M–$34M band is the practical hospital-fundraising range called out for the portfolio.

Dollar figures are concept projections (pilot assumptions: ~1,000 customers/day/location, ~20% donation rate; national scale from the mini plan). Actual results depend on participation, franchise adoption, and partner agreements. Vision in earlier materials: ~95% of net program revenue to RMH and children’s hospitals. Any use of McDonald’s, Ronald McDonald House, or hospital names/marks requires appropriate permission.

LONG-TERM VISION

Start in St. Louis. Grow nationwide.

Launch first at St. Louis Children’s Hospital — where the idea was born — then expand to children’s hospitals across the country. If one retail partner does not adopt it, the same donation-to-hospital-kitchen model can adapt to other family-friendly chains.
