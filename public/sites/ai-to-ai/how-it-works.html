The fundamentals

How the clone works, then how it calls.

Three layers. Build them in this order. Layer 1 has to exist before a phone ever rings. Layer 2 is the first useful product. Layer 3 is AI to AI.

Layer 1 — Clone you

Make a private vault, not a public repo

Everything about you stays locked: résumés, Google Docs exports, the 15 companies, how you talk. That is Personal Axon. Putting it on a public GitHub repo would give the world your life. Joe never gets a copy of the vault, and he never gets a key to it.

Load the facts once

Upload the files. Talk it through. “This is A1. This is Pre-De-Icer. This is how I describe SiteEye.” The clone answers like you, inside guardrails you set.

Joe learns from the clone, not from a sit-down

When you want Joe to understand the companies, you grant his Axon a narrow door: it may ask yours about the work you named. You can close that door. He is not “logged into Martin.”

Layer 2 — Your Axon calls a person

This is the pizza, the doctor, and “call Joe, how much to seal 5,000 square feet.”

You give one instruction

Out loud or typed: “Call Joe at this number. Ask the cost to seal a 5,000 square foot parking lot. Then tell me.” One job. No extras unless you said so.

It places the call on your line

The call goes out through your Zadarma PBX number, so it looks like a real outbound call from you, not a random robot pool. Your Axon is the one talking.

It says who it is

“Hello, I’m Martin Simpson’s AI team member. He asked me to check one thing.” It does not pretend to be you in person. That keeps the other side honest and keeps you out of trouble.

It asks, listens, and stops

It asks the question. If they say “do you want anchovies?” it can answer from your instruction. If they wander, it comes back to the one job. Then: “Thank you. We’ll get back with you.”

It reports to you, not through you

When the call ends, you get a text or an email: what was asked, what they said, anything still open. You did not have to sit on the line. You did not have to type back and forth with the shop.

Payment stays off this layer for now. A credit card on a phone call is a later, locked step, with a one-time limit (“pay only for this pizza”). Do not store cards in the clone.

Layer 3 — Your Axon talks to their Axon

Same instruction from you. No phone. Two brains, both authorized.

Each person has their own Axon

Yours knows you. Joe’s knows asphalt, prices, and your jobs together. Neither vault is shared.

You both say yes, once

A pair is a permission, not an API key in Joe’s pocket. “Joe’s Axon may ask Martin’s Axon about sealing quotes.” Either of you can revoke it.

Your Axon sends the ask

You still just say what you want. Your Axon packages it and sends it to Joe’s Axon. No texting the human unless a human is needed.

Their Axon answers, or taps them

If Joe’s Axon knows the price, it answers. If it does not, it chimes Joe — text, email, or a ping — and waits. Then it sends the result back to yours, and yours tells you.

Layer 2 still matters even after Layer 3 exists. Pizza shops and doctor offices will not all have an Axon. The clone calls a person when there is no other brain to talk to. Same instruction from you either way.

What this is not

Not a public clone

The brain is not a website anyone can open. It is not a GitHub dump of your Google Docs.

Not handing Joe a key

Authorization is a door you open and close. It is not “here is my API key.”

Not you still in the middle

If you are still typing every reply to the shop, it failed. The Axon finishes the job, then tells you.

Next build

Layer 1 is Personal Axon, already on this site. Layer 2 is the first phone call through Zadarma, one number, one scripted ask, a text back. Layer 3 comes after two brains exist.
