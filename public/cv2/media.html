In print

St. Louis Business Journal — “Pied Piper of Maplewood,” by Margaret Jackson. October 26, 2003.

A cover profile of Marty’s plan for Sutton Boulevard in Maplewood. The reporter’s opening line: “Marty Simpson plans to transform Sutton Boulevard in Maplewood into a children’s entertainment district.”

What the article documents:

  • He had spent close to $4 million over roughly six years acquiring Maplewood property, and planned to invest several million more.
  • The plan was a family- and children-oriented entertainment district — he was in discussions with a children’s theater organization, a circus company, and a restaurant operator about joining it.
  • The stated inspiration was his 3-year-old daughter, Mia, who had come through neurosurgery, radiation, and chemotherapy for a brain tumor.
  • Second floors of some commercial buildings were to become condominiums, with sale revenue subsidizing very low rent for the children’s organizations and bringing residents back downtown. He had also bought an apartment building overlooking the Sutton Loop to convert to upscale condominiums.
  • Maplewood’s population was shifting toward young families with children.
  • His Maplewood investment was financed largely with money made developing real estate in Florida.
  • Background as described: a California native who came to St. Louis in the early 1980s for Washington University; HIV counseling work; and Specialized Testing, then about ten years in Maplewood, contracting with Quest Diagnostics and Laboratory Corporation of America to provide health-screening testing.

Cited, not reproduced — the article is the Business Journal’s copyright. The surviving online reproduction ends partway through, so the original print piece may have carried more.
