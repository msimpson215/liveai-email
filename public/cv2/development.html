Historic Downtown Maplewood — Property Investment, Redevelopment & Philanthropy

Martin acquired and owned substantial commercial property throughout historic downtown Maplewood, Missouri — including Manchester Road and Sutton Boulevard. This was much more than a single Sutton Boulevard development: it was real estate, restaurants, entertainment venues, and space for community institutions. Martin wrote the entire downtown of how it was going to work, because he could already see it. He laid out the plan with a fellow restaurateur — where the restaurants would sit, how the district would feel. The vision came from California Avenue in Palo Alto, California, where he grew up.

Maya Cafe was a restaurant he founded and owned in Maplewood, and Momo’s followed. He developed and built The Focal Point music venue and owned the building; the nonprofit organization that runs it was not his. He retired from Maplewood development in 2006. Maya Cafe is still open today, and the district is still thriving.

  • Nearly $4 million: Maplewood property acquisitions documented by the St. Louis Business Journal in 2003.
  • Approximately $3.4 million: the later reported sale of the Maplewood property portfolio. This is separate from the businesses — it was not a sale of Specialized Testing Services, Specialized Counseling, Medical Testing Solutions, or Axon AI.

Contributions to Maplewood

Long-term, affordable space is what lets arts and entertainment organizations stay in a historic downtown. Martin owned the buildings, so he could lease them to the Black Cat Theater, The Focal Point, and Pywacket Children’s Theater for $1 a month — keeping theater, live music, and children’s arts in Maplewood and drawing people to the district.

Black Cat Theater

He provided the Black Cat Theater a building valued at approximately $1 million. The building was leased for $1 per month — leased, not donated.

The Focal Point Music Venue

He developed and built The Focal Point music venue and owned the building, not the nonprofit organization. He gave The Focal Point a 20-year lease for $1 per month, so the venue had an affordable, permanent home for live music in Maplewood.

Pywacket Children’s Theater

He gave Pywacket Children’s Theater a permanent home in his Maplewood building on a 20-year lease for $1 per month.

Friends of Kids with Cancer

He leased Friends of Kids with Cancer the most visible space in his building for a penny a month. After he retired from Maplewood, the later owners of the building ended that lease.

The St. Louis Business Journal covered the plan on October 26, 2003 under the headline “Pied Piper of Maplewood.” By then he had spent close to $4 million over about six years buying property in historic downtown Maplewood, including along Manchester Road and Sutton Boulevard, with several million more planned. The article names the inspiration plainly: his three-year-old daughter Mia, who had come through neurosurgery, radiation, and chemotherapy. He was in talks with a children’s theater organization, a circus company, and a restaurant operator about joining the district, and the second floors of several commercial buildings were to become condominiums — the sales subsidizing rent low enough that children’s organizations could actually afford Sutton Boulevard. He had also bought an apartment building overlooking the Sutton Loop to convert. Much of it was financed with money he had made developing real estate in Florida. Full citation on the Media page.

He still wanted to do for the Sutton Loop what Joe Edwards did for the University City Loop. The KETC Channel 9 profile on the Media page is the clearest statement of what he was trying to do — and why giving back to kids became the mission.

Maplewood and kids

After his daughter Mia was diagnosed with a PNET (primitive neuroectodermal tumor), everything shifted — personally and professionally. He had always tried to give back; at the hospital he felt helpless about her pain and got close to a lot of the other kids on the floor. Maplewood came after that turn. As profiled by KETC Channel 9, he created a family entertainment district across from the old Sutton Loop — Pywacket kids’ theater got a permanent home on a twenty-year lease for a dollar a month, and Friends of Kids with Cancer got the best spot in the building for a penny a month.

His line: seeing people at restaurants having a great time is good — but you cannot forget that somewhere a kid is fighting for their life at a hospital.
