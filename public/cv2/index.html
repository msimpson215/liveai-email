Career · Where It Began

Upbringing & Education

Before the companies and the AI, there was Palo Alto and Washington University.

Born at Stanford Hospital

Palo Alto, California

Martin was born at Stanford Hospital and raised in Palo Alto, where he went to grade school, middle school, and Palo Alto High School. He grew up there before and during the creation of Silicon Valley.

What shaped him most was the community itself: open-minded, intellectual, and right next to Stanford. The friends he still keeps in touch with from those years all recognize what a beautiful place it was to grow up.

Class of 1987

Washington University in St. Louis

Martin came to St. Louis from California to attend Washington University on a full tuition scholarship. He earned degrees in biology and sociology and graduated in 1987.

He was headed toward neuroscience. That is where the name Axon comes from.

Career · Three Decades of Building Companies

Career

After Washington University, Martin went into public health during the HIV/AIDS crisis, at Doorways and the Missouri Department of Health. People were waiting two weeks for their test results. So in 1993 he organized the first coast-to-coast rapid HIV/STI laboratory network. That became a nationwide medical testing company, and the career that followed.

Products & Patented Inventions

Alongside his companies, Martin invented physical products, each one built to fix a specific real-world problem. His first, Pre-De-Icer, earned U.S. Patent #8,119,025.

U.S. Patent #8,119,025

Pre-De-Icer™

Patented salt-free preventive anti-icing formulation applied before winter storms so frost, snow, and ice cannot bond to windshields, equipment, or road surfaces.

Pediatric & Community Work

Children’s Hospitals & Community

Much of Martin’s work has been for children. It started on the pediatric oncology floor at St. Louis Children’s Hospital, and it shaped the Maplewood district and the Ronald McDonald Kitchen proposal.

Happy Meals on Saturdays

Built custom heated and cooled wagons to deliver individualized Saturday meals across the pediatric oncology floor at St. Louis Children’s Hospital — the direct inspiration for the Ronald McDonald Kitchen proposal.

Support for Children’s Organizations

Provided dedicated Maplewood space and long-term support for Pywacket Children’s Theater and Friends of Kids with Cancer.

Family Entertainment District

Designed a downtown district centered around children and families, documented by PBS KETC Channel 9.
501(c)(3) Hospital Concept

Ronald McDonald Kitchen

Proposed restaurant-to-hospital giving model funding children’s hospitals and placing a short, familiar free menu in hospital cafeterias for families in treatment. Independent concept proposal; not affiliated with McDonald’s or RMHC.
