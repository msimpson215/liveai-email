What defines him

If you watch one thing, watch this. KETC Channel 9 — Living in St. Louis on Maplewood, Mia, and the turn toward giving back — Pywacket kids’ theater, Friends of Kids with Cancer, and a family district built for kids, not around a slogan.

Watch Channel 9 feature

Maplewood and kids

After his daughter Mia was diagnosed with a PNET (primitive neuroectodermal tumor), everything shifted — personally and professionally. He had always tried to give back; at the hospital he felt helpless about her pain and got close to a lot of the other kids on the floor. Maplewood came after that turn. As profiled by KETC Channel 9, he created a family entertainment district across from the old Sutton Loop — Pywacket kids’ theater got a permanent home on a twenty-year lease for a dollar a month, and Friends of Kids with Cancer got the best spot in the building for a penny a month.

His line: seeing people at restaurants having a great time is good — but you cannot forget that somewhere a kid is fighting for their life at a hospital.

Children’s Hospital Initiatives & Philanthropy

These are programs Martin personally founded and organized for children in pediatric oncology at St. Louis Children’s Hospital.

Happy Days on Saturday

Martin founded Happy Days on Saturday at St. Louis Children’s Hospital. Every Saturday he organized meal deliveries for children in pediatric oncology — approximately 70–80 meals each week. The children could request whatever they wanted. This was his personal philanthropic initiative, not a corporate sponsorship.

Children’s Toy Chest

Martin founded the Children’s Toy Chest for pediatric oncology patients, coordinated with Friends of Kids with Cancer. Its purpose was simple: to give children going through cancer treatment toys and comfort during long hospital stays.

Maplewood Arts & Community Organizations

In Maplewood he owned the buildings and leased them for $1 a month: a building valued at approximately $1 million to the Black Cat Theater (leased, not donated), and a 20-year lease to The Focal Point music venue, whose building he developed and owned. Full details on the Development page.
