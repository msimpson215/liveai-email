HIV, patients, and a national lab network

This is the HIV chapter. He fought for — and succeeded in creating — a national network of patients served through multiple regional labs in the United States. Not a clinic chain he owned in every city. A network: patients, labs that already existed, and a way to reach them.

He advocated for virtual medicine when that was not a slogan. In 1994 he invested in a legal fight that lasted a year and a half. This page does not invent the caption of the case. It records that he put his own money and time into it, and that the fight lasted eighteen months.

Specialized Testing Services — the first national network

Before him, there was no coast-to-coast STAT testing network you could call into. Marty built it: roughly six regional laboratory relationships, patient service centers nationwide, counselling included, results in days instead of weeks. Annual gross sales about $1.4 million.

Quest later tried to run the same play nationally and, in his account, could not pull it off the way he did — he knew the counselling, the labs, and the phone work as one system. That is the business he is proudest of from this chapter.

Medical testing center

From that lab-and-PSC pattern he built medical testing companies, including Medical Testing Solutions — nationwide DNA paternity testing run as a call center out of the St. Louis metro, using LabCorp and other collection sites. Marketing was in-house geo-SEO rather than expensive pay-per-click, so the test could be priced below typical internet competitors.

Annual gross sales on this medical testing work were about $4 million — not the smaller figure that sometimes shows up on old drafts.

Restaurants

He owned and operated restaurants, including My Cafe (the Maya Cafe storefront on Sutton), Momo’s, and Boogaloo’s in Maplewood. Momo’s opened with a partner he later stepped back from in the first month. That operating history also informs the later fundraising proposal for kids’ hospitals: a restaurant-based giving model, not a speech.

This page does not invent menus, cities, or restaurant revenue. The names are part of the record; the rest belongs in a conversation if you want it.
