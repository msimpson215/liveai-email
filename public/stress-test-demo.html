Concept — Patient Question Line

“Here's a code. It explains the test, whenever you want.”

The tech gives the same careful explanation she always gives. Then she hands over a card, so the patient still has it at nine that night when the questions actually show up.

The card she hands them

Questions about your stress test?

Point your phone camera at the code, then just talk. Ask it anything about the test — here, or later at home.

Camera → point → tap → talk

This code is live. Scan it with your phone right now.

What the patient does

  1. Points the phone camera at the card. No app, no download, no login.
  2. A page opens. One big circle in the middle.
  3. Taps it and talks like a phone call. It answers out loud.
  4. Keeps the card. Asks again next week if they forgot.

What it explains

  • What is this actually looking for? Blood flow to the heart at rest versus working hard — narrowing that only shows up under demand.
  • What are they doing to me? The IV, the tracer, the camera, the stress portion, the second set of pictures.
  • What does the Lexiscan feel like? Flushing, shortness of breath, headache, metallic taste — and that it passes in about fifteen minutes.
  • Why no caffeine? It blocks the medication, which can ruin the images and force a repeat.
  • Why did nobody tell me anything? The tech isn't the one who reads it. A quiet tech is not bad news.
  • When do I hear something? Usually days to about a week, through the ordering doctor.
  • Where do I go, what do I bring? Suite, floor, phone number, arrival time, insurance card, medications in bottles.

What it refuses to do

  • No medical advice, opinions, or recommendations of any kind.
  • Never reads or interprets results. No numbers, no scans, no “is that bad.”
  • Never touches medications — including whether that coffee was a problem.
  • Never assesses personal risk or says the test is safe “for you.”
  • Symptoms right now → stops and says get a nurse. Nothing else.
  • No internet access and no memory. It cannot wander off script or retain anything about anyone.
  • Asks for no personal information. No name, no birthdate, no chart number.
