Re-evaluating the Upfront Payment and Deal Structure
Given your health concerns and your desire for a larger, more immediate payout, the focus of
the negotiation should be shifted away from long-term royalties and towards a more substantial
upfront payment. This is a common strategy, and it is entirely possible to negotiate a deal that
reflects this priority.
Here's a more in-depth look at how to approach this, with a more optimistic view of the numbers:
1. The Value Proposition Is Not Just the Bandage
The value of your invention is not just the physical dissolvable bandage; it's the new platform
and brand category it creates. You are not just selling a better bandage; you are selling a new
way of thinking about wound care for both children and adults with fragile skin.
For a company like Johnson & Johnson, a "Dissolvable Band-Aid" is not just a new product. It is
a potential billion-dollar product line that can revitalize an entire brand and capture a new
generation of customers. The value of this innovation is what you should be emphasizing in your
pitch.
2. Restructuring the Deal for a Larger Upfront Payment
A typical licensing deal is often structured with a balance of upfront, milestone, and royalty
payments to spread the risk for the licensee. However, a negotiation can heavily favor the
upfront payment.
● Selling the IP vs. Licensing: You could, in theory, negotiate to sell the patent outright
for a single, large lump sum. This would be a clean break from the invention, and you
would receive all the money at once, but you would give up any future royalty payments.
● Larger Upfront, Smaller Royalties: A more common approach to prioritize upfront cash
is to negotiate for a much larger upfront payment in exchange for a lower royalty rate.
For example, instead of a $1 million upfront payment and a 6% royalty, you might
negotiate a $5 million upfront payment with a 2% royalty rate.
