We were recently approached by the father of a cancer survivor, Martin Simpson, about a concept he’s carried in his heart for years. Now that his daughter is cancer-free, he’s ready to fulfill that dream — and in doing so, it has become our dream too.

During his daughter’s treatment, Martin noticed that while the hospital cafeteria offered truly exceptional food, the children lit up when they received something familiar — like a McDonald’s Happy Meal. Each Saturday, for over three months, he brought his custom-made wagon — equipped with a battery-operated cooler and two warmers — to the Oncology Department, delivering each child exactly what they had ordered earlier that day. If a child wanted no pickles, they got no pickles. His goal was simple: to make sure the children ate and felt remembered. The Happy Meal was always a hit.

This effort — which the children affectionately called “Happy Meals on Saturdays” — sparked a bigger idea: what if this could grow into a full-time initiative that supports not just the children, but also the institutions that care for them?

With the help of McDonald’s Corporation and their longstanding commitment to children, along with St. Louis Children’s Hospital and Ronald McDonald House, we believe this initiative — now called Ronald McDonald Kitchen — can bring joy to thousands and raise significant revenue for these trusted organizations.

Our vision is to operate a small McDonald’s kiosk inside the hospital, fully managed and staffed by McDonald’s Corporation to ensure quality, consistency, and safety. 95% of all net revenue would go directly to the Ronald McDonald House and Children’s Hospital. The enclosed flowchart outlines how this sustainable model would work, starting in St. Louis — with the potential to grow nationally in the years to come.

Our mission is simple: to help children eat well, feel seen, and experience comfort while undergoing treatment. A Happy Meal might seem small, but it can be a huge source of joy and nourishment — both physical and emotional — for a child and their family. Even the hospital staff has expressed how uplifting this tradition was during its original run.

To help bring this to life, we plan to engage major corporations throughout the St. Louis area to sponsor the kiosk — offering both public recognition and a way to directly impact families in crisis. Marketing and community engagement will be spearheaded by Natalie, who brings a passion and focus to making sure the message reaches the hearts of donors and supporters alike.

We are fully committed and ready to move forward.
