My opinion? If your goal is eventually to sell the company, I'd lean toward a
subscription/service model, but not a pure lease.
I'd structure it like this:
SiteEye LIVE™ Service
Customer pays:
● Initial setup fee
● Monthly service fee
They receive:
● Hardware
● Cellular connectivity
● Dashboard access
● Support
● Software updates
Why?
Because companies that buy businesses often pay higher multiples for recurring revenue than
for one-time hardware sales.
Example
Company A:
● Sells 500 units at $2,000 each.
● Makes revenue once.
● Must constantly find new customers.
Company B:
● Has 500 units deployed.

-- 5 of 12 --

● Generates $89/month per unit.
● Has predictable recurring revenue.
Acquirers usually find Company B more attractive because they can forecast cash flow.
But...
I would still offer an outright purchase option.
Not because you want people to choose it.
Because some contractors hate subscriptions.
So:
Option 	Basic 	Pro
Service 	$399 setup + $89/mo 	$999 setup + $119/mo
Purchase 	$1,999 	$3,999
If 80–90% choose the service, great.
If a contractor insists on ownership, you don't lose the sale.
What I'd do in the first 12–24 months
Honestly?
Don't over-engineer the business model yet.
Build:
1. One prototype.
2. Put it in Joe's hands.

3. Let Joe use it on real jobs.
4. Let Tony beat up the economics.
Then you'll learn things you can't predict today:
● Do crews tolerate it?
● Does it survive wind and weather?
● Does Joe actually check it?
● Is $89/month a bargain or too much?
● What breaks first?
Those answers will drive the business model more than any spreadsheet.
If Joe uses it for 30 days and says:
"I can't imagine running my crews without this now."
That's when you start thinking seriously about patents, manufacturing, and whether you're
building a hardware company, a software company, or a service company.
Right now, if I had to choose one path with the information we have:
Service/subscription first, purchase option second.
That's the model I'd bet on if the long-term goal is building value and eventually selling the
company.
