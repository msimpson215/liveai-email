A Concept Born from Experience and Hope

We were recently approached by the father of a childhood cancer survivor, Martin Simpson, about a heartfelt concept he’s held onto for years. Now that his daughter is cancer-free, he hopes to fulfill that dream and it’s quickly become ours as well.

During his time at St. Louis Children’s Hospital, Martin noticed something important. While the cafeteria food was exceptional, the children in the oncology unit found something uniquely comforting in having a Happy Meal from McDonald’s. Each Saturday for months, he would pull a custom-made wagon, outfitted with a battery-powered cooler and warmers, up to Nine West Oncology.. Every child got exactly what they ordered: if a child didn’t want pickles because chemo drugs made them sick eating them, then they would get no pickles., If a child craved McNuggets over a cheeseburger, no problem, they get McNuggets..  No order was ever refused.

Beyond the emotional comfort, his deeper motivation was to help the kids keep eating for their strength, their nutrition, and their recovery.

The impact of those simple meals was unforgettable. The kids started calling it “Happy Meals on Saturdays.” And from that love, a bigger idea was born.

Martin saw the opportunity not just to deliver meals but to raise real revenue for the very people and institutions that helped save his daughter’s life and so many others: St. Louis Children’s Hospital and Ronald McDonald House Charities. We believe that, through the power of McDonald’s brand and its long-standing commitment to children, we can bring this idea to life in a way that’s meaningful, scalable, and deeply impactful.

The materials enclosed outline the concept, but we would be thrilled to sit down and share the full vision with you.

A few important notes:

We would never use your name or literature or suggest any affiliation with McDonald’s Corporation, Ronald McDonald House, or St. Louis Children’s Hospital without full permission.

This idea is shared in good faith from a father who cares and wants to make a difference.

If you choose to pursue it, full control(suggesting a 501c3) would be in your hands.

The working name for this project has been “Ronald McDonald Kitchen,” a humble tribute to the warmth and care symbolized by the Ronald McDonald House name. We imagined a logo with a storybook-style potbelly stove, with little hearts rising from the flue using McDonald's color scheme. All of which symbolize comfort, familiarity, and nourishment.

We’d love to meet with you, introduce Martin, and see if this dream can become a reality. We sincerely believe it could generate needed revenue, but mainly bring hope and happiness to thousands of children and families starting right here in St. Louis.
